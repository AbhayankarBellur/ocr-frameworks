<b>Scaled Dot-Product Attention</b>
3.2.1
We call our particular attention "Scaled Dot-Product Attention" (Figure 2). The input consists of
queries and keys of dimension <math>d_k</math>, and values of dimension <math>d_v</math>. We compute the dot products of the
query with all keys, divide each by <math>\sqrt{d_k}</math>, and apply a softmax function to obtain the weights on the
values.
In practice, we compute the attention function on a set of queries simultaneously, packed together
into a matrix <math>Q</math>. The keys and values are also packed together into matrices <math>K</math> and <math>V</math>. We compute
the matrix of outputs as:
Attention<math>(Q, K, V) = \operatorname{softmax}(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}})V</math>
(1)
The two most commonly used attention functions are additive attention [2], and dot-product (multi-
plicative) attention. Dot-product attention is identical to our algorithm, except for the scaling factor
of <math>\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}}</math>. Additive attention computes the compatibility function using a feed-forward network with
a single hidden layer. While the two are similar in theoretical complexity, dot-product attention is
much faster and more space-efficient in practice, since it can be implemented using highly optimized
matrix multiplication code.
While for small values of <math>d_k</math> the two mechanisms perform similarly, additive attention outperforms
dot product attention without scaling for larger values of <math>d_k</math> [3]. We suspect that for large values of
<math>d_k</math>, the dot products grow large in magnitude, pushing the softmax function into regions where it has
extremely small gradients <sup>4</sup>. To counteract this effect, we scale the dot products by <math>\frac{1}{\sqrt{dx}}</math>.
